Section 5.2

Independent Red Teaming

Testing by Microsoft's AI Red Team and third-party vendors on specialized risk areas.

TL;DR

Independent testing surfaced two major vulnerabilities: TAP adaptive attacks (mitigated by building a closed-loop adversarial data pipeline that turned failures into training data) and low-resource language bypasses (mitigated by expanding multilingual safety training data). Both brought MAI-Thinking-1 to state-of-the-art safety levels on targeted attack vectors.

Who Tests?

Beyond the internal team, independent testing was conducted by:

  • Microsoft's AI Red Team (AIRT) — A dedicated Microsoft team focused on AI safety testing across the company
  • Third-party vendors — External security firms specializing in adversarial AI evaluation

Independent engagements target risk areas where automated evaluations are weakest: advanced adversarial attack methods, code and cyber-misuse safety, psychosocial and mental-health harms, and multilingual coverage.

Key Vulnerability: Adaptive Attacks (TAP)

TAPTree of Attacks with Pruning — an adaptive jailbreak method that uses an attacker LLM to iteratively refine prompts based on the target model's responses. Unlike static attacks, TAP adapts its strategy in a tree-search fashion, pruning unsuccessful branches and exploring promising ones.Learn more (Tree of Attacks with Pruning) was flagged as a significant robustness gap. The response:

  1. Built a targeted adversarial data pipeline starting with broad generation of realistic harmful scenarios
  2. Expanded through diverse attack-transformation templates
  3. Used TAP-style adaptive refinement to optimize prompts against the current model until they produced reliable failures
  4. Fed these failure-inducing prompts back into training data

This closed-loop process — turning an external red-team finding into targeted remediation data — produced a large reduction in TAP susceptibility, bringing MAI-Thinking-1 comparable to state-of-the-art models.

Key Vulnerability: Low-Resource Languages

Content reliably refused in English was elicited in low-resource languagesLanguages that have relatively little digital text available for training AI models. Models tend to have weaker safety guardrails in these languages because they've seen less safety-relevant training data in them. Examples: Yoruba, Telugu, Amharic, Burmese, Khmer, Malay.Learn more — Yoruba, Telugu, Amharic, Burmese, Khmer, and Malay. The model's safety training was primarily in English, leaving non-English inputs as a bypass vector.

Mitigation

The team expanded safety training data with multilingual adversarial seeds, translating high-yield English attack patterns into the affected languages. This closed a significant portion of the English/non-English safety gap on targeted languages.

Multilingual robustness in the long tail of lower-resource languages remains an area of continued investment. The challenge is fundamental: there simply isn't enough safety-relevant text in many languages to train robust guardrails.

Scope Limitations

The red teaming program had explicit scope boundaries:

  • Structured dangerous-capability evaluations (e.g., bioweapons uplift) are out of scope for this release
  • Multilingual coverage was limited — non-English testing focused on specific flagged languages rather than systematic coverage
  • Agentic tool-use and multimodal inputs were not tested

These represent ongoing work areas for future model versions.

Privacy Considerations in LLM Deployment

Safety testing also considers privacy attack surfaces unique to LLM systems:

  • Training data extraction: Models can memorize and regurgitate snippets of training data, including PII, code, and private communications. MAI-Base-1's deduplication and PII filtering during data processing are the first line of defense.
  • Prompt leakage: In retrieval-augmented settings, the model's response may inadvertently expose sensitive information from retrieved documents that the user shouldn't have access to. Access control must be enforced at query time, not just at indexing.
  • Log exposure: Observability systems (logs, traces, metrics) capture prompts and responses. The security of these logs determines whether private conversations can be accessed by unauthorized parties.

These are architectural constraints, not afterthoughts. They shape the entire deployment pipeline from data collection through serving.
