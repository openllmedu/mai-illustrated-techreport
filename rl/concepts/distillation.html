Concept Deep-Dive

Self-Distillation

How the model learns from its own outputs to recover from crashes, carry forward progress, and upgrade base models.

The Problem

RL training for reasoning takes thousands of steps over weeks. During that time, several things can go wrong:

  • Numerical instabilities — Small discrepancies between training and inference compound and cause the model to diverge
  • Crashes — Hardware failures, OOM errors, and infrastructure issues interrupt training
  • Better base models — A new, improved pre-trained checkpoint becomes available mid-run

Simply resuming from a checkpoint before the crash often doesn't work — instabilities can be embedded in the model parameters many steps before the actual collapse.

How Self-Distillation Works

  1. Collect traces: During RL training, save the model's successful reasoning traces (the chain-of-thought that led to correct answers)
  2. Curate: Filter for quality — use traces from later stages of the climb (better reasoning), ensure prompt diversity, and optionally remove degenerate CoTs
  3. Retrain: Take a fresh mid-trained checkpoint and train it on the collected traces via SFT
  4. Resume RL: Use the SFT-trained model as the new starting point for continuing the RL climb
Recovering from a crash: The STEM climb has been running for 2,000 steps when a numerical instability causes divergence. The team has been saving traces throughout. They take traces from steps 800-1,900 (skipping early and unstable-late steps), train a fresh base model on 1M selected traces, and resume RL from this new checkpoint. Within ~200 additional RL steps, the model surpasses where it was before the crash.

Key Lessons Learned

How Many Traces?

About 1 million is the sweet spot. More traces provide diminishing returns and risk over-constraining the model — a narrowed output distribution leaves less room for exploration when RL resumes.

Which Traces?

Later-stage traces are better than early-stage. But using only the final checkpoint's traces is worse than using traces from multiple strong checkpoints. The diversity of reasoning strategies from different checkpoints helps subsequent RL exploration.

Correct-Only or All?

Including traces that led to incorrect answers performs similarly to correct-only. But since the RL runs produce far more than 1M successful traces, the team ultimately uses only correct traces.

Diversity Over Quantity

For a fixed token budget, more diverse prompts beat more traces per prompt. Simple random sampling outperforms biased selection strategies (shortest-trace, heuristic filtering).

Long-Context Preservation

Self-distillation can cause the model to forget long-context abilities learned during mid-training, especially when traces come from earlier RL stages with shorter max lengths. Fix: mix mid-training data with reasoning traces before length extensions.

Self-distillation is not just a recovery mechanism — it's a core part of the training pipeline. It enables parallel development (try risky experiments, keep the good parts), base model upgrades mid-run, and format changes (e.g., switching from raw text prompts to chat format).
