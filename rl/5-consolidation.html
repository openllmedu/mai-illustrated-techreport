Section 3.5

Consolidation

Merging three specialist models into MAI-Thinking-1 through distillation and final RL.

TL;DR

Three specialist models (STEM, Agentic, Helpfulness) are merged into one via two stages: SFT on combined reasoning traces (56% STEM, 11% Agentic, 33% Helpfulness by sample count, trained for 4 epochs), followed by lightweight RL to refine safety and style while retaining a small STEM/coding data mixture to prevent reasoning degradation.

Why Consolidation?

By this point, the team has three separate specialist models, each excellent at their domain but trained independently. The final product — MAI-Thinking-1 — must be a single model that excels at all three domains simultaneously. Consolidation merges them in two stages.

Stage 1: Consolidation SFT

The team collects successful reasoning traces from all three specialists and trains a single model on the combined data via SFTSupervised Fine-Tuning — training the model to reproduce specific outputs by standard next-token prediction. Faster and more stable than RL, used here to "distill" knowledge from all three specialists into one model.Learn more.

Why the huge token imbalance? STEM/coding reasoning traces are much longer (often 10k-100k tokens of step-by-step reasoning), while helpfulness responses are typically short (200-2000 tokens). By sample count, the split is 56/11/33 — by token count, STEM dominates at 89%. The team found this token imbalance does not harm helpfulness capabilities.

Key differences from standard self-distillation:

  • Each specialist uses different filtering: STEM/agentic use light filtering across multiple checkpoints; helpfulness uses LLM judges for style and defect detection
  • Training runs for 4 epochs (standard self-distillation uses 1-2)
  • Learning rate decays by 2x from a maximum of 1e-5

Stage 2: Consolidation RL

After SFT, a final lightweight RL climb further improves safety, reduces over-refusals, and refines style. This stage is based on the Helpfulness & Safety climb recipe with two important additions:

  • Training at the full 128k token max sequence length
  • A small proportion of STEM and coding data is retained in the RL mixture

Both are critical: without 128k training, complex reasoning degrades; without STEM data, reasoning performance slowly erodes during the helpfulness-focused RL.

The consolidation strategy (specialist diverge, then converge) lets the team iterate on each domain independently in parallel, then combine. This is much faster than training a single model on everything from scratch — each specialist can be developed by a separate team on separate GPU clusters.

Avoiding Catastrophic Forgetting

A fundamental risk in multi-phase training: each new phase can overwrite capabilities learned in previous phases. When the helpfulness RL runs, it could degrade STEM reasoning. When consolidation SFT runs, it could lose agentic problem-solving skills.

The team uses several mitigations:

  • Data mixing: Always include some data from previous phases during new training. The consolidation RL retains STEM/coding problems to prevent reasoning erosion — without this, math accuracy measurably declines within 100 steps of helpfulness-only RL.
  • Low learning rate: Consolidation uses 1e-5 or lower — 100x smaller than pre-training (1e-4). Small updates preserve existing capabilities while adding new ones.
  • Self-distillation as a reset: If a capability is lost during RL, the team can self-distill from a checkpoint where the capability was intact, then resume. This acts as a "save point" system.
  • Continuous monitoring: STEM, agentic, helpfulness, and safety benchmarks are evaluated continuously during all training phases. Any regression is caught within tens of steps.
Example of forgetting in practice: During Stage 2 helpfulness RL, the team noticed AIME math accuracy dropped from 82% to 76% over 200 steps — the model was "forgetting" how to do multi-step mathematical reasoning as it optimized for conversational helpfulness. Adding 15% STEM problems to the helpfulness RL data mixture recovered accuracy to 81% within 100 steps while helpfulness metrics continued improving.
